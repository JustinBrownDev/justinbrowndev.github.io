AGGRESSIVE INFINITE CHUNK STREAMING - HOT STREAM PATCH
=====================================================

Observed problem
----------------
Real-browser boot log showed the authored spawn generator accelerating from
roughly building 10/38 to 37/38 in about 0.4 seconds, while the supposedly much
lighter post-handoff infinite stream appeared to crawl or stop.

Root cause
----------
The generic chunk factory was already fast, but it inherited multiple nested
cooperative yield points. Each ~millisecond chunk could be forced to wait for
several requestAnimationFrame boundaries during its internal 7x7 construction,
and the world streamer then forced another yield after commit. Wall-clock wait
vastly exceeded actual generation cost.

Correction
----------
- Generic chunk construction is now atomic and off-scene.
- Visuals and physics still publish together in one atomic commit.
- No unconditional per-chunk requestAnimationFrame sleep remains.
- The OUTER world pump is now the only post-handoff CPU scheduler.
- Pump stops between complete chunks when its real millisecond budget is spent.
- Urgent render-ring policy: up to 24 chunks / 12 ms per pump.
- Prefetch policy: up to 16 chunks / 8 ms per pump.
- Warm maintenance: up to 6 chunks / 4 ms per pump.
- Warm cooldown is zero; movement can immediately create new work.
- Console instrumentation prints [world-perf] with pump chunk count, pump ms,
  average chunk build ms, and render/prefetch completion.

Exact-seed synthetic throughput check
-------------------------------------
Seed: 671278205 (the seed from the supplied Firefox log)
Factory: real infinite-city-chunks.js with vendored Three.js, real chunk
streamer, off-scene build + atomic scene/physics commit.

48 procedural chunks built/committed (7x7 neighborhood minus spawn):
- total build CPU: ~75.5 ms
- average build: ~1.57 ms/chunk
- average commit: ~0.006 ms/chunk
- 5x5 render ring: 25/25 complete after ~65.9 ms measured CPU/wall work in Node
- 7x7 prefetch ring: 49/49 complete after ~79.5 ms total measured work

These numbers are not a browser FPS promise; they prove the generator itself is
cheap and the previous slowdown was scheduler-imposed waiting.
